## 0. Governing principles

Everything below is subordinate to three rulings. When a task here conflicts with one of them, the ruling wins.

**P1 — Apply, don't reorganise.** AMCR and TEATER remain authoritative in how their terms are organised and interpreted. Nothing in this workstream constitutes a change to either source vocabulary.

**P2 — Facets are plumbing.** They organise and prioritise the prompt. They are not semantic output and not a new taxonomy. Keep "which terms are available to the model" strictly separate from "how those terms are grouped for inference".

**P3 — Keep by default; exclude on evidence.** A TEATER branch comes out only when enrichment results show it is irrelevant or introduces systematic, otherwise unsolvable noise. Purely technical/administrative AMCR lists are the standing exception. **M11 applied this to the a-priori exclusions and they are back;** whether any goes out again is now an evidence question, and §5 is largely about making that evidence cheap to read.

M6's context estimate from 26 August — *"~4 689 terms ≈ 57.9k tokens against 128k+
windows"* — was made for exactly this scope and landed within **0.5 %** of the built
result.

**Three regressions were predicted, and pinned in the same commit.** TEATER 288's ids
sort below the content branches (605 < 2424, 1022 < 1685, 602 < 2250), so `olej`,
`vodní pramen` and `úřední písemnost` — the terms the B5 fix rescued — would each have
been captured by the reinstated record and dragged out of Artefact/Feature. Glosses are
identical in all three, so only the facet was ever in question. A test now pins all four
B5 terms, because this is a mechanism and the next harvest can produce more.

**The guardrail pairing is a mechanism, not a convention.** `geo_guardrail` declares
whether the clause is in force and which rules it reaches; `PROMPT_GEO_GUARDRAIL`
selects the wording; `vocab_build.py` renders the selected block and refuses a build
where the two disagree. All six pairings were exercised — only the three coherent ones
build.

**@david-spacil's qualifier question is answered and enforced.** Two records sharing a
`qualifier_cs` build the same key, and the second is dropped with its id reaching no
`discarded_ids` — silently, because a warning is not a failure. He had read it exactly
right, and his convention (qualify only the record that leaves the group) is now a
`validate_settings` rule, along with the mirror fault of qualifying every member.

`conflicting` groups were splits, against 2–6 % everywhere else. **Sort by
`aat_verdict`, and do not repeat the "~30 rows" claim.**

## 4. Workstream D — the whole remaining critical path

D1 is the only thing between this vocabulary and a number anyone should act on, and the
reinstatement made every one of its questions larger:

| Question | What it decides                                         | Scale then → now                      |
|----------|---------------------------------------------------------|---------------------------------------|
| **D1-a** | partial credit for a less-specific term                 | 914 → **3 225** pairs (68 % of terms) |
| **D1-b** | either side of a composite pair                         | 98 → **102** pairs                    |
| **D1-c** | is `teater_category_ids` scored, or only the label      | 470 (23 %) → **509** (11 %)           |
| **D1-d** | a bracketed homonym vs its bare label                   | 1 → **7** live splits                 |
| **D1-e** | how the two keyword fields and meta-text are reported   | unchanged                             |
| **D1-f** | a context term where the gold label is a find — **new** | **2 638** terms, two facets           |

**D1-f is not deferrable.** With 2 638 context terms selectable and the prompt now
merely *preferring* an archaeological one, a drop in accuracy after the rebuild could
equally mean the model got worse or that 2 638 new distractors entered the enum — and
nothing in a single headline number distinguishes them. Per-facet accuracy does, which
is the second reason the facet layout matters.

### 4.1 The corpus: present, untracked, and now stale

`git ls-files data_samples/DOC_LINE_CATEG data_samples/UDP` returns **6 files, all
synthetic**; the 19 real documents / 2 172 lines exist only on one machine.

**New this revision:** the corpus-derived sheets are also **stale**.
`corpus_review.meta.json` records `vocabulary_single_word_terms: 1017` and
`terms_with_hits: 101` — computed against the 2 074-term vocabulary. Against 4 718 terms
those numbers are simply wrong, and they cannot be refreshed here: `corpus_review.py`
correctly refuses to overwrite sheets built from a larger corpus than the one on disk.

**So there is now a concrete, blocking task with a name:** on the machine that holds the
corpus, re-run `python3 corpus_review.py --all` and commit the refreshed sheets — and
settle, in the same breath, whether the corpus itself is committed or published as a
checksum + provenance record. Until then `corpus_term_evidence.csv` and
`corpus_branch_evidence.csv` describe a vocabulary that no longer exists.
